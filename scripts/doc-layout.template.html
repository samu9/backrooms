How the Backrooms layout is generated

The algorithm in src/gen.ts and src/chunk.ts. The pictures on this page come from the real generator code, bundled into this file.

TL;DR
  • The world isn't stored. solid(gx, gz) is a pure function of the seed and a 0.5 m cell coordinate, so it's infinite, the same every time, and chunks can be built in any order.
  • The plane is cut into zones (~26 m) with borders bent by noise. Each zone picks a type (office, maze, corridor, hall, weird, void) with its own wall spacing, door odds and ceiling height.
  • Inside a zone, walls run along lines every S cells. Each segment between two crossings exists or not, can have a door, and in weird zones can be partial. Blocks can get an extra split wall, and halls get pillars.
  • Grids from different zones are never reconciled where they meet. That clash is what makes the layout feel wrong.
  • Each chunk turns the grid into merged wall and ceiling quads and an occupancy texture. That's 2 draw calls per chunk.

1 · One hash, no state

Every random choice goes through a single 32-bit integer hash (src/gen.ts:23):

hash3(x, y, salt) → [0, 1)
  h = x·374761393 + y·668265263 + (salt + seed·1013)·2246822519   (mod 2³²)
  h = (h ^ h>>13)·1274126177;  h ^= h>>16;   return h / 2³²

A decision is always hash3(integer coordinates, the salt for that decision) < probability. Because the inputs are integers and the arithmetic wraps at 32 bits, JavaScript (Math.imul) and GLSL (uint) get the same bits. The shaders use the same hash for flicker timing and ceiling stains.

What solid(gx, gz) does (src/gen.ts:176). It answers in a few dozen integer operations, with no memory of neighbouring cells.

2 · Zones: deciding what kind of space you're in

The plane is divided into 52-cell (26 m) zones. Square zones would be easy to spot, so before the cell is snapped to a zone, its coordinates are pushed by 1-D value noise along the other axis (src/gen.ts:142):

wx = gx + floor(14 · noise(gz · 0.035))
wz = gz + floor(14 · noise(gx · 0.031))
zone = (floor(wx / 52), floor(wz / 52))    → cached params for that zone

Each zone hashes its own coordinates to pick a type and parameters (makeZone, src/gen.ts:68):

Every zone also gets a random grid offset off ∈ [0, S) and has a 30% chance of a wooden chair rail on its walls.

3 · Lines, segments, doors

Inside a zone, walls can only sit on wall lines: one-cell-thick (0.5 m) lines every S cells in both directions, shifted by off. Two lines cross at a crossing, and the piece of line between two crossings is a segment. Each segment is decided on its own:

  1. Exists? hash(line, segment, zone+10) < p (or pz for lines along x). Corridor zones set one to 0.93 and the other to 0.1, which gives long parallel halls.
  2. Door? With probability pd, an opening dw cells wide is cut at a hashed position, never touching the crossing.
  3. Partial? In weird zones, each segment keeps only the hashed range [a, b], so walls stop in the middle of a room.
  4. Crossings are wall if any of the four segments meeting there exists, so a junction never floats on its own or leaves a pinhole.
  5. Split (in blocks with S ≥ 6): with probability split, a block gets one extra wall across it at a hashed position, with its own door. This turns a regular grid into uneven rooms and closets.
  6. Pillars (hall and void zones): the corner of a block, 1×1 or 2×2 cells, becomes solid with probability pillar. These are the forests of columns.

4 · Why it feels wrong (on purpose)

Neighbouring zones are never reconciled. Where a maze with S = 4 meets an office with S = 14 and a different offset, each cell simply follows its own zone's rules. The result:

  • walls that end flush against a wall from another grid, or a cell short of it;
  • corridors that open into a pillar hall at an odd offset, and slivers of room one cell wide;
  • the ceiling jumping between 2.2 m and 6 m in the middle of a room, because height belongs to the zone, not to the room. The chunk builder turns that jump into a soffit face.

Press jump to a zone border in the explorer to see one. Nothing checks that the layout makes sense, and nothing makes sure every space is reachable (see gotchas).

5 · Ceiling lights

Panels sit on a fixed 3 m lattice that ignores zones (lightKind, src/gen.ts:222):

  • A panel covers 1.2×0.6 m. If any of the 8 cells under it is wall, there's no panel.
  • 5% are missing, 8% are dead, and 7% flicker (the flicker timing is hashed in the shader). On top of that, about 8% of 5×5-panel blocks (15 m) are entirely dead. Those are the dark areas.
  • Each chunk bakes the states of the 16×16 panels around it into a tiny texture. The fragment shader reads the 4×4 nearest panels and softens their contribution towards the edge of that window, so no seam shows as the set of panels changes.

6 · From cells to triangles

A chunk is 64×64 cells (32 m). buildChunk (src/chunk.ts) samples solid() and the ceiling height over a 96×96 window: the chunk plus a 16-cell border on every side.

  • Walls (src/chunk.ts:144): for each cell edge the chunk owns, a face is emitted where wall meets open space (height = the open cell's ceiling) or where two open cells have different ceilings (a soffit, facing the taller side). Runs of identical faces are merged into one quad.
  • Ceilings (src/chunk.ts:126): open cells are greedily merged into rectangles of equal height.
  • Floor: one quad for the whole chunk. Walls hide the floor underneath them.
  • Occupancy texture: the 96×96 window is uploaded as RGBA8 (wall, ceiling height, chair rail). Because of the border, the shader can see the neighbouring chunks' walls for soft shadows and corner darkening.
  • Collision, the minimap and the screenshot viewpoints all call the same solid(), so what you see, bump into and map always matches.

Gotchas

No guarantee every space is reachable. Door odds are high enough that you're almost never sealed in, but it can happen, most often in maze zones. Noclip (N) is the escape hatch, and it fits the setting.
The hash exists twice. hash3 in gen.ts and in shaders.ts must stay identical, including the seed·1013 salt. Otherwise flicker and stains won't match between CPU and GPU.
Floor division everywhere. Negative coordinates need Math.floor and a true modulo (((a % b) + b) % b). Using |0 or % on its own mirrors the grid around the origin.
The zone cache is only a speed-up. Zone parameters are cached because solid() runs about 9k times per chunk build. The cache is cleared when the seed changes. Correctness never depends on it.
Edge ownership. A chunk emits wall faces only for cell edges 0..63 on each axis. Edge 64 belongs to the next chunk, so faces are never doubled or missing at chunk borders.
